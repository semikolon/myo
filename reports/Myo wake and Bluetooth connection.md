# Coax two dormant Myos back onto Bluetooth

A Myo that lights on the charger but stays dark when unplugged and shaken has most likely run out of usable battery, not lost its radio. Thalmic's own LED table gives "both LEDs off" exactly three meanings: normal sleep (cured by moving it), "turned off" deep sleep (cured by plugging in and unplugging USB), and a battery at 0 %. Shaking rules out the first, and the charger visit you have already made should have cured the second, which leaves cells that either never filled or cannot carry the load once USB is removed. That also explains the empty bleak scan: a Myo advertises only while it is awake, unplugged and unconnected, and Thalmic built it to be "non-operational while plugged in", so neither a charging band nor a dark one will ever show up. Three free checks settle the diagnosis before any money is spent: the status LED colour on the charger (pulsing orange versus solid green, and how fast it turns green), whether a USB drive named "Myo" mounts on the Mac, and whether the Thalmic logo pulses blue in the first seconds after unplugging. If a band does wake, connecting from macOS needs no dongle: a bleak scan filtered on the control service UUID `d5060001-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842`, run from Terminal.app, finds it, and a handful of bytes written to one characteristic keep it awake and report battery and firmware. If the cells are dead, two small 3.7 V LiPo cells per band are sold as replacements, but nobody has published a replacement walkthrough.

## A Myo has three dark states, and a shake answers only one

Thalmic documents a normal idle sleep: "The Myo armband automatically enters a battery-saving sleep mode when it is idle for a short period of time. It will exit sleep mode when you pick it up" ([Thalmic support, Wayback 2016](https://web.archive.org/web/20160801023928/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/204167679-How-do-I-turn-off-the-Myo-armband-)). Thalmic never states the timeout; community users put it at **about 30 seconds** without activity when the band is not synced ([myo-python issue #36](https://github.com/NiklasRosenstein/myo-python/issues/36)). Waking needs only motion picked up by the IMU, not skin contact or EMG; wearing the band matters only for the sync gesture, and **a synced Myo never sleeps** ([Thalmic LED table, Wayback 2016](https://web.archive.org/web/20160909133119/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/202724369-What-are-the-different-LED-status-descriptions-for-the-Myo-armband-)).

The second state is "turned off", which is the protocol's deep sleep. Myo Connect's Armband Manager offered "Turn Myo Off - Turn Myo off completely to prepare it for travel or an extended sleep" ([Thalmic support, Wayback 2017](https://web.archive.org/web/20171108204254/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/205031939-Using-the-Armband-Manager-in-Myo-Connect)), and the published BLE header defines the same thing as command `0x04`, "Put Myo into deep sleep" ([myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h)). There is no power button, and **only USB power leaves this state**: "In order to turn Myo back on, you must connect it to your computer or a wall charger through the micro USB cable" ([Thalmic support](https://web.archive.org/web/20160801023928/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/204167679-How-do-I-turn-off-the-Myo-armband-)). Thalmic's reset is the same act, plug in and then unplug, with no minimum time stated ([Thalmic support: reset, Wayback 2019](https://web.archive.org/web/20190719060714/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/201769279-How-to-reset-the-Myo-armband)). Thalmic documents no separate factory shipping mode. The third dark state is an empty battery, also cured only by USB.

The band is deliberately inert on USB: it "is specifically designed to be non-operational while plugged in". While charging the logo LED is off and the status LED pulses orange; it turns solid green when full, and **a full charge takes 2 to 3 hours** on any standard micro-USB charger ([Thalmic support: charging, Wayback 2016](https://web.archive.org/web/20160801023714/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/202649593-How-do-I-charge-the-Myo-armband-)). Thalmic rates the battery at "a day of continued use" and "up to a week" in unconnected standby ([Thalmic support: battery life, Wayback 2019](https://web.archive.org/web/20190717173148/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/202641403-Battery-life-of-the-Myo-armband)); community libraries report that deep sleep lasts months ([MioConnect](https://github.com/francocruces/MioConnect)). After years in a drawer, whichever sleep the bands were left in, their cells are empty.

Thalmic's complete LED table, verbatim in meaning ([Thalmic LED table](https://web.archive.org/web/20160909133119/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/202724369-What-are-the-different-LED-status-descriptions-for-the-Myo-armband-)):

| Logo LED | Status LED | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Off | Pulse orange | On power, charging |
| Off | Constant green | On power, fully charged |
| Pulse blue | Off | Awake, not connected, not synced; sleeps after a short period |
| Constant blue | Off | Not connected, synced; will not sleep |
| Pulse blue | Constant blue | Connected over Bluetooth, not synced |
| Constant blue | Constant blue | Connected and synced |
| Flash blue | Constant blue | Warming up; vibrates when done |
| Constant blue | Flash orange | Low battery |
| Off | Off | Asleep (move it), turned off (USB), or battery at 0 % |
| Off | Pulse purple | Completing a firmware update |
| Off | Flash red | Error; reload the firmware |
| Off | Constant red | Error; contact support (no user fix exists) |

A solid orange LED is also listed by Thalmic as a symptom of an unresponsive band needing a firmware reload ([Thalmic support: unresponsive, Wayback 2016](https://web.archive.org/web/20160801023636/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/202198919-My-Myo-armband-is-unresponsive-won-t-turn-on-or-displays-a-solid-orange-LED)).

## The Myo advertises only while awake, unplugged and unconnected

The state to look for is **logo pulsing blue, status LED off**. That is a band that is awake and free, and it is the only state in which a scan will find it. A macOS-first project used with real Myos puts it plainly: "The Myo advertises over BLE only while it is awake", and after an unplug and a nudge "the logo glows blue, it is now advertising for ~30 s" ([OSA MYO Controller guide](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/guide.md)). A band with its status LED constant blue is connected to some other host, an old phone or a BLED112 dongle elsewhere, and will not advertise; Thalmic's own setup guide reads a blue status LED that way ([Thalmic support: Getting Started, Wayback 2017](https://web.archive.org/web/20170704034009/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/210708483-Myo-not-connecting-via-Bluetooth-during-Getting-Started-Guide)). No primary source says in one sentence that the Myo stops advertising on USB power; that follows from "non-operational while plugged in" and the logo LED being off while charging, and a scan with a band plugged in versus just unplugged would confirm it in a minute.

The advertisement carries the control service UUID as AD type `0x06` (an incomplete list of 128-bit service UUIDs), placed last in the packet, which three independent byte-level matchers rely on ([myo-raw](https://github.com/dzhu/myo-raw/blob/master/myo_raw.py), [pyomyo](https://github.com/PerlinWarp/pyomyo/blob/main/src/pyomyo/pyomyo.py), [Open-Myo](https://github.com/Alvipe/Open-Myo/blob/master/open_myo.py)). It also carries a local name, which is "Myo" unless the owner renamed the band in Myo Connect, in which case the custom name is advertised ([Open-Myo issue #6](https://github.com/Alvipe/Open-Myo/issues/6)). bleak's CoreBluetooth backend reads both fields, and a 2025 user confirmed that `BleakScanner.discover(service_uuids=["d5060001-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842"])` "can discover my Myo" ([pymyo issue #4](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/issues/4)). Matching on the UUID **or** the name, as OSA does, catches renamed bands ([OSA bleak_transport.py](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/src/myo_bridge/transport/bleak_transport.py)).

No open-source Myo library on any platform performs BLE bonding, pairing or encryption before reading and writing the characteristics ([OSA](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller), [dl-myo](https://github.com/iomz/dl-myo), [pymyo](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo)). The original dongle is therefore not needed and holds nothing the Mac must reproduce; Thalmic itself said developers could "connect to built-in Bluetooth adapters using the Bluetooth Protocol" ([Thalmic support, Wayback 2019](https://web.archive.org/web/20190717164800/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/203996655-Can-I-use-my-computers-built-in-Bluetooth-)). One user could not connect from a Mac until the battery had drained to 2 or 3 % and guessed the band had dropped an old pairing ([FreeMyo.py](https://github.com/jshahbazi/FreeMyo.py)); that is a single anecdote its author marks as uncertain, and it bears on connecting, not on an empty scan.

So the five-minute scan failed for one of two reasons. If the bands were on the charger during it, they were in their designed non-operational state. If they were unplugged and dark, they were not awake. A filter problem is unlikely, provided it matched the UUID or the name rather than a remembered address, which CoreBluetooth never exposes ([bleak scanner docs](https://bleak.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/scanner.html)).

## Your symptom points to the cells, and three free checks settle it

A Myo merely asleep or turned off would have woken when it came off the charger, because the unplug is itself the documented reboot, and its logo would then pulse blue. Yours stay dark, and the remaining branches are a battery at 0 % that is not filling, a cell that charges but collapses under the boot load once USB is gone, or a boot that failed at the USB disconnect, which Thalmic acknowledges happens: the band "may fail to boot after connecting or disconnecting from a computer via USB" ([Thalmic support: unresponsive](https://web.archive.org/web/20160801023636/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/202198919-My-Myo-armband-is-unresponsive-won-t-turn-on-or-displays-a-solid-orange-LED)). No field report from 2020 to 2026 describes this exact pattern with a confirmed cause, so the following ladder is built from Thalmic's documented states rather than from someone who fixed the same fault.

| What you see | What it means | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Status pulses orange for many hours and never turns green | The charger never sees the cell reach full: a failed or disconnected cell | Inspect for swelling; plan on new cells |
| Green within minutes, dark the moment it is unplugged | The charge path works but the cell holds almost nothing | Replace the cells |
| Green after roughly 2 to 3 h, logo pulses blue after unplug | Healthy enough to test | Scan at once and keep the band moving |
| Green, dark after unplug, but a "Myo" drive mounts on the Mac | Board alive; boot or firmware fault, or weak cell | Reflash 1.5.1970, then retest |
| Status flashes red | Firmware error | Reflash via the "Myo" drive |
| No "Myo" drive on the Mac | MCU not enumerating | Thalmic's fix is to drain fully, then reconnect |

The **charger colour** is the cheapest discriminator, because Thalmic gives it only two charging meanings. Give each band a long, uninterrupted charge, well past the nominal 2 to 3 hours, before judging it: many LiPo charger chips feed a deeply discharged cell a small conditioning current before charging normally. That is general LiPo behaviour; no source names the Myo's charger chip, and **no one has reported reviving a years-dead Myo by extended charging, or failing to**. A cell over-discharged for years can be unsafe to recharge, so look first for a pod that bulges or a gap opening between pod halves.

The **"Myo" drive** is the second free test. Plugged into a computer, the band mounts as a removable drive named "Myo", served by the main Kinetis MCU on bus power, so the drive appearing proves the board boots even if the cells are dead ([Thalmic support: manual firmware update, Wayback 2016](https://web.archive.org/web/20160909214528/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/201763319-How-do-I-manually-update-the-firmware-on-my-Myo-armband-)). Use a port on the Mac itself, not a powered hub, and a cable known to carry data, since Thalmic's own troubleshooting names damaged cables and hubs first ([Thalmic support: not charging, Wayback 2019](https://web.archive.org/web/20190717172200/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/208807646-Myo-armband-is-not-charging-or-turning-on)). A 5 V wall charger that lights the band tells you nothing about the drive. Reflashing needs no Myo Connect: copy the HEX file onto the drive, wait for it to remount with a `SUCCESS.txt`, unplug, and the status LED pulses pale purple while the update finishes. The last firmware, `myo-firmware-1.5.1970.hex` (1,009,572 bytes), survives as a release asset on GitHub after Thalmic's S3 links began returning 403 ([myo-python release v1.0.4](https://github.com/NiklasRosenstein/myo-python/releases/tag/v1.0.4)); a 2025 macOS user upgraded from firmware 0.8.18 this way and called it easy ([pymyo issue #5](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/issues/5)). Treat any mirrored HEX as an untrusted download and prefer that GitHub release over aggregator sites. If the drive will not mount, Thalmic's procedure is to let the battery drain completely, which "can take 2 - 3 days", then reconnect ([Thalmic support: not recognized through USB, Wayback 2016](https://web.archive.org/web/20160801023732/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/206475205-Myo-armband-not-recognized-through-USB)); for a band that is already flat, that condition is met.

The **first seconds after unplugging** are the third test. Watch the logo: pulsing blue means awake and advertising, nothing at all means the cells or the boot failed.

Battery *health*, as opposed to charge state, cannot be read from a register. Once connected, the standard battery characteristic `0x2A19` reports only a percentage ([myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h)); health shows in how fast that percentage falls. Against Thalmic's rating of a day of continued use, a band that loses tens of percent in its first idle hour after a full charge is far below spec. That threshold is my inference from the rating, not a published figure.

If the cells are dead, each band holds **two LiPo cells in separate pods**, behind electrodes, reached by cutting through the rubber gasket that joins the pods ([Adafruit teardown](https://learn.adafruit.com/myo-armband-teardown/inside-myo)). A 2018 review paper lists them as 3.7 V, 260 mAh each ([Visconti et al., 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/324889539_Technical_Features_and_Functionalities_of_Myo_Armband_An_Overview_on_Related_Literature_and_Advanced_Applications_of_Myoelectric_Armbands_Mainly_Focused_on_Arm_Prostheses)), while the aftermarket part sold as replacing Myo 571830 / 144440100156 is 3.7 V, 220 mAh, 32 × 17 × 6 mm, about 10 to 12 euro ([Volterino CS-MYG100RC](https://www.volterino.com/CS-MYG100RC.html)). The figures conflict, so measure an original cell before ordering. Nobody has published a completed replacement, whether the cells are wired in parallel, or whether they use connectors or solder; replacing both cells together is the safe choice, and the band will not be as neat or sealed afterwards.

## Connecting from macOS 26 takes Terminal, bleak and a few bytes

Run the scanner from Terminal.app or iTerm2. Any process that uses CoreBluetooth must be launched by an app that declares Bluetooth usage, or macOS kills it silently; an IDE or an AI desktop app's built-in shell can crash on `scan` with no message ([OSA protocol notes](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/protocol.md), [OSA hardware tests](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/hardware-tests.md)). If your earlier scan listed other devices, permission and radio already work. CoreBluetooth gives each peripheral a per-Mac UUID instead of its MAC address, so select a band by that UUID, its name, or the serial number read after connecting; the MAC printed on the box can be compared against the 6-byte serial in the info characteristic ([Thalmic support: MAC address, Wayback 2019](http://web.archive.org/web/20190719061207/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/207523646-Finding-the-MAC-address-of-a-Myo-armband)). If a band later shows up in a scan but connections time out, macOS may hold a stale pairing: forget the Myo in System Settings → Bluetooth and toggle Bluetooth off and on. That fault shows as connect timeouts, never as an empty scan ([OSA guide](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/guide.md)).

Two libraries suit the Mac Mini. **OSA MYO Controller** is macOS-first, native on Apple Silicon, reports use with physical Myos over the built-in radio, and offers `osa-myo scan` and `dump` (commits to 2026-10-22, macOS 12+, Python 3.11+); its own hardware checklist cautions that nothing counts as verified until you run it ([OSA README](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller)). **pymyo** is a small bleak wrapper exposing battery, firmware, sleep mode, vibration and LEDs, tested only on firmware 1.5.1970 ([pymyo](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo)). dl-myo also claims macOS support, but its discovery uses bleak's undocumented `retrieveAddressForPeripheral_` path and it has an open CoreBluetooth write error ([dl-myo issue #22](https://github.com/iomz/dl-myo/issues/22)); pyomyo and myo-raw need the BLED112 dongle. No library documents a test on macOS 26.

The GATT protocol is small. Every Myo UUID has the form `d506XXXX-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842`; commands are `[id, payload_length, payload…]` written to the command characteristic `0x0401`. Write `09 01 01` (never sleep) first so the band stays awake for the session, read firmware from `0x0201` as four little-endian u16 values (a retail band should read 1.5.x, hardware revision 2), read battery from the standard `0x2A19`, and write `09 01 00` on exit so the band can sleep again ([myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h), [pymyo myo.py](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/blob/main/pymyo/myo.py)). The header claims big-endian values, but every working implementation uses little-endian ([OSA protocol notes](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/protocol.md)). **Never send `04 00`** (deep sleep) while experimenting: after it only USB brings the band back. The sketch below follows those byte layouts and has not been run against a band:

```python
import asyncio, struct
from bleak import BleakScanner, BleakClient

CONTROL  = "d5060001-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842"
FIRMWARE = "d5060201-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842"
COMMAND  = "d5060401-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842"
BATTERY  = "00002a19-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb"

def is_myo(dev, adv):
    name = (adv.local_name or dev.name or "").lower()
    return CONTROL in (adv.service_uuids or []) or "myo" in name

async def main():
    dev = await BleakScanner.find_device_by_filter(is_myo, timeout=60)
    if dev is None:
        print("No Myo awake"); return
    async with BleakClient(dev) as c:
        await c.write_gatt_char(COMMAND, bytes([0x09, 0x01, 0x01]), response=True)
        print(dev.address, dev.name)
        print("firmware", struct.unpack("<4H", await c.read_gatt_char(FIRMWARE)))
        print("battery %", (await c.read_gatt_char(BATTERY))[0])
        await c.write_gatt_char(COMMAND, bytes([0x09, 0x01, 0x00]), response=True)

asyncio.run(main())
```

Start it, then unplug the band and keep it moving until the script reports a device; with the roughly 30-second advertising window, starting the scan first matters. A phone app such as nRF Connect or LightBlue is a useful cross-check because it removes macOS and library variables, but it cannot see a band that is dark ([FreeMyo.py](https://github.com/jshahbazi/FreeMyo.py)).

## Conclusion

The question that looked like a Bluetooth problem is a power problem: an empty scan follows directly from the Myo's designed rule that it advertises only when awake and off USB, and your bands never reached that state. Charging lights prove that the charge circuit and the main chip get power; they say nothing about whether the cells hold charge. The decisive evidence costs nothing and takes an afternoon: how long each band takes to go green, whether the "Myo" drive mounts, and whether the logo pulses blue after unplugging. Once a band does wake, the dongle is irrelevant, because no pairing or bonding stands between the Mac and the band.

Two outcomes are worth planning for. If a band wakes, the battery percentage it reports while idling for an hour is the honest measure of how much life its cells have left, and that number, not the charging light, decides whether these bands are usable as they are. If they stay dark with a mounting drive and a quick green, the board is alive and the fault is the cells; given cheap replacement cells and no published replacement guide, documenting that repair would itself be a first for a discontinued device that academics already describe as gradually becoming nonfunctional ([von Arnim et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3771594.3771626)).
