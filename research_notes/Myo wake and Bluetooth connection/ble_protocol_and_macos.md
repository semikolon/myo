# Myo armband: BLE advertising, GATT protocol, and connecting directly from macOS

Research date: 2026-11-02. Library sources were read from shallow clones of each repository on that date (commit dates given per library), not from memory. Thalmic's support site is gone; its pages were read from Wayback Machine snapshots (2016–2019).

## When does the Myo advertise? (awake, charging, bonded)

### Takeaway
The Myo advertises only while it is awake, unplugged, and not connected to another host. Thalmic designed it to be "non-operational while plugged in", so a Myo on the charger (status LED pulsing orange, logo LED off) is not expected to advertise at all, which by itself explains a scan that runs while the bands charge. To wake one: unplug it (plugging in and unplugging is also Thalmic's official "reset"), then move or tap it. The logo then pulses blue and it advertises until it sleeps again after a short idle period.

### Cited Findings
- Thalmic's charging FAQ: "The Myo armband is not intended to be worn while plugged in (e.g., while charging the battery) and is specifically designed to be non-operational while plugged in." The LED pulses orange while charging and goes solid green when full; a full charge takes 2–3 h. — [Thalmic support: How do I charge the Myo armband? (Wayback 2016)](http://web.archive.org/web/20160801023714/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/202649593-How-do-I-charge-the-Myo-armband-)
- Thalmic's LED table: while charging, the **logo LED is off** and the status LED pulses orange. When fully charged, the logo is off and the status LED is solid green. **Logo pulsing blue, status off** = "not connected by Bluetooth and the Sync Gesture has not been performed. Myo will enter sleep mode after a short period of time." **Logo constant blue, status off** = not connected, synced: "Myo will not enter sleep mode while synced." **Both LEDs off** = (1) sleep mode, "Move Myo to wake it"; (2) "turned off. Connect to a power source via USB to turn on"; or (3) battery at 0 %. Status LED flashing red = firmware error, "Reload the firmware"; constant red = contact support. — [Thalmic support: LED status descriptions (Wayback 2016)](http://web.archive.org/web/20160909133119/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/202724369-What-are-the-different-LED-status-descriptions-for-the-Myo-armband-)
- Thalmic's reset procedure is to connect the Myo to a computer with micro-USB and then disconnect it. — [Thalmic support: How to reset the Myo armband (Wayback 2019)](http://web.archive.org/web/20190719060714/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/201769279-How-to-reset-the-Myo-armband)
- If a Myo is unresponsive, will not boot after USB connect/disconnect, or shows a solid orange LED: plug it in. If the USB drive named "Myo" mounts, copy the firmware HEX file onto it. If it does not mount, let the battery drain completely, reconnect, wait for the "Myo" drive, and reload the firmware. — [Thalmic support: unresponsive / solid orange LED (Wayback 2016)](http://web.archive.org/web/20160801023636/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/202198919-My-Myo-armband-is-unresponsive-won-t-turn-on-or-displays-a-solid-orange-LED)
- OSA MYO Controller's guide (a macOS project, Oct 2026): "The Myo advertises over BLE only while it is **awake**. A Myo that has been idle sleeps and stops advertising; it wakes up when it is moved, tapped, or plugged into USB. The *deep sleep* state (entered when the battery dies or when a tool sends the deep-sleep command) can only be left by plugging it into USB." Its steps: charge, then "Unplug and move it: the logo glows blue, it is now advertising for ~30 s", then scan. — [OSA-MYO-Controller docs/guide.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/guide.md)
- The same guide: "A Myo connected to another host (an old Myo Connect, a phone) does not advertise. Quit that app first." Its hardware checklist says to check that `scan` does not list a Myo that is connected to another app. — [OSA guide.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/guide.md); [OSA docs/hardware-tests.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/hardware-tests.md)
- The OSA checklist says to wake the band by plugging it into USB for a second or tapping the logo: "the logo pulses blue while it advertises". — [OSA docs/hardware-tests.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/hardware-tests.md)
- `deep_sleep` (command 0x04): pyomyo says "the 0x04 command puts the Myo into deep sleep, there is no way to completely turn the device off". OSA says that after deep sleep "only USB wakes the band". — [pyomyo source](https://github.com/PerlinWarp/pyomyo/blob/main/src/pyomyo/pyomyo.py); [OSA docs/protocol.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/protocol.md)
- Pairing with the dongle in Myo Connect was by proximity: "Tap the Thalmic Labs logo on your Myo armband to the USB Bluetooth adapter." Up to three Myos could be connected at once. — [Thalmic support: Pair and connect a new Myo (Wayback 2019)](http://web.archive.org/web/20190717171738/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/208807366-Pair-and-connect-a-new-Myo-Armband-in-Myo-Connect)
- No open-source Myo library on any platform (myo-raw and pyomyo over the dongle; pymyo, dl-myo, OSA, FreeMyo over bleak; Open-Myo over bluepy) performs BLE bonding, pairing or encryption before reading and writing the GATT characteristics. A source search for "bond/pair/encrypt" in dl-myo, pymyo and OSA finds no such code (OSA's only "bond" field is a BGAPI scan-response field). — source search of the cloned repos ([dl-myo](https://github.com/iomz/dl-myo), [pymyo](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo), [OSA](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller))
- FreeMyo's troubleshooting suggests that when the correct UUID still will not connect, you drain the battery: "I don't know if it keeps any information about previous pairings, but it will drop that info when the battery is drained enough… when the battery got down to 2 or 3%, I was able to connect". — [FreeMyo.py README](https://github.com/jshahbazi/FreeMyo.py)

### Inferences
- The most probable explanation for the failed 5-minute scan, **if the bands were on USB during the scan**, is that a charging Myo is by design not operational: logo LED off, not advertising. Next test: unplug both bands, tap or shake each one until the logo pulses blue, and scan straight away. The advertising window is short (OSA says ~30 s) unless the band is synced or a client sets never-sleep after it connects.
- If an unplugged, moved band shows **no LED at all**, it is in deep sleep, flat, or turned off. All of these are left only through USB power: plug in, then unplug, then move.
- A **flashing or solid red** status LED, or a band that never pulses blue after an unplug, points to a firmware fault. The USB mass-storage "Myo" drive path (copy the HEX file) is the documented recovery and needs no Myo Connect.
- No source shows that the Myo stops advertising because it is *bonded* to an old dongle. The evidence is consistent with plain one-central-at-a-time behaviour: it does not advertise while a connection is open, and no bonding is required. The "drain the battery to clear pairings" claim is one person's anecdote, which its own author marks as uncertain.

### Gaps
- No primary source says outright that the Myo stops BLE advertising while on USB power. This is inferred from Thalmic's "non-operational while plugged in" and the logo-LED-off state while charging. A scan with the band plugged in versus unplugged would settle it in a minute.
- No primary source gives the exact advertising interval, or the idle timeout before sleep. The "~30 s" figure comes only from OSA's guide.
- Whether Myo firmware keeps a bond table that a Myo Connect "pairing" wrote is undocumented. Thalmic's developer blog (developerblog.myo.com) was not in the Wayback index under a Bluetooth URL.

## What is in the advertisement, and does filtering on the control service UUID work in CoreBluetooth?

### Takeaway
The advertisement carries the Myo control service UUID `d5060001-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842` as an AD structure of type 0x06 (Incomplete List of 128-bit Service UUIDs), placed last in the packet. It also carries a local name, "Myo" or the name the owner set in Myo Connect. Sources show no manufacturer data. Both a service-UUID filter and a name filter work with bleak on macOS, so a correct filter would have found an awake Myo. The empty result points to the band's state, not to the filter.

### Cited Findings
- myo-raw (dongle, 2017) and pyomyo (dongle) identify a Myo by the end of its raw advertisement payload: `p.payload.endswith(b'\x06\x42\x48\x12\x4A\x7F\x2C\x48\x47\xB9\xDE\x04\xA9\x01\x00\x06\xD5')`. That is AD type 0x06 followed by the 16 UUID bytes in little-endian order, so the UUID list is the last AD structure. — [myo-raw myo_raw.py line ~211](https://github.com/dzhu/myo-raw/blob/master/myo_raw.py); [pyomyo pyomyo.py line ~266](https://github.com/PerlinWarp/pyomyo/blob/main/src/pyomyo/pyomyo.py)
- Open-Myo (bluepy, Linux) scans for the same AD entry: `if j[0] == 6 and j[2] == '4248124a7f2c4847b9de04a9010006d5'`. A user in issue #6 found that newer bluepy formats the value as `'d5060001-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842'` (still type 6). The same scan data also had AD type 8 (Shortened Local Name) holding the name set "when i initialized the MYO through official software". — [Open-Myo open_myo.py](https://github.com/Alvipe/Open-Myo/blob/master/open_myo.py); [Open-Myo issue #6](https://github.com/Alvipe/Open-Myo/issues/6)
- `myohw.h` defines `MYO_SERVICE_INFO_UUID` as these bytes (`42 48 12 4a 7f 2c 48 47 b9 de 04 a9 01 00 06 d5`), which is the control service UUID d5060001-…, as `kMyoServiceInfoUuid`. It states: "the full service UUID for GCControlService would be d5060001-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842." — [thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h)
- OSA (macOS, CoreBluetooth via bleak) states: "Discovery: the band advertises the control service UUID `d5060001-…` (complete 128-bit list) and a local name (`Myo` or the user-set name). We treat a device as a Myo if either matches." Its filter `_looks_like_myo` returns true when `adv.service_uuids` contains the control UUID **or** the name contains "myo". The README says it is "Used with physical Myos over the Mac's built-in Bluetooth". — [OSA docs/protocol.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/protocol.md); [OSA bleak_transport.py](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/src/myo_bridge/transport/bleak_transport.py); [OSA README](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller). The AD type OSA gives ("complete") conflicts with the type 0x06 ("incomplete") that the three byte-level matchers above rely on.
- pymyo's maintainer suggested `BleakScanner.discover(service_uuids=["d5060001-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842"])` to find Myos; a user replied "Yes, it can discover my Myo". The issue does not name the platform. — [pymyo issue #4 (Sept 2025)](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/issues/4)
- dl-myo's `Myo.with_uuid()` matches `GATTProfile.MYO_SERVICE = "d5060001-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842"` against `adv.service_uuids`, using `BleakScanner.find_device_by_filter(..., cb=dict(use_bdaddr=True))`. — [dl-myo myo/core.py, myo/profile.py](https://github.com/iomz/dl-myo/blob/main/myo/core.py)
- bleak's CoreBluetooth backend builds `service_uuids` from `kCBAdvDataServiceUUIDs`, the name from `kCBAdvDataLocalName` and manufacturer data from `kCBAdvDataManufacturerData`, and also applies the `service_uuids` filter in Python (`is_allowed_uuid`). It warns that "macOS 12.0, 12.1 and 12.2 require non-empty service_uuids kwarg, otherwise no advertisement data will be received". The latest bleak release is v3.0.2 (2026-05-02). — [bleak corebluetooth/scanner.py](https://github.com/hbldh/bleak/blob/develop/bleak/backends/corebluetooth/scanner.py); [bleak releases](https://github.com/hbldh/bleak/releases)
- The Web Bluetooth demo's `requestDevice` filters accept either `{name: this.name}` or a device that advertises the battery, IMU, control **and** EMG service UUIDs. — [myo-web-bluetooth.js](https://github.com/charliegerard/myo-web-bluetooth.js/blob/master/myo-web-bluetooth.js)

### Inferences
- Because three independent byte-level implementations match AD type 0x06, the advertisement most likely carries an *incomplete* 128-bit list. CoreBluetooth reports such UUIDs in `kCBAdvDataServiceUUIDs` as well, which is consistent with OSA and pymyo #4 finding Myos with a UUID or name filter. A UUID **or** name filter (as in OSA) is the robust choice for bands that were renamed in Myo Connect.
- A Myo renamed in Myo Connect advertises that custom name, not "Myo". A name-only filter for "myo" can therefore miss a renamed band, but the UUID filter still catches it.
- The Web Bluetooth demo's service filter requires four UUIDs in the advertisement. If only the control UUID is advertised (as the byte matchers suggest), only its name filter can match. This is a plausible cause of its "Not working" issue, but that is not verified.

### Gaps
- No captured raw advertisement (flags, TX power, whether the name is in the advertisement or in the scan response) from a firmware 1.5 band was found in a primary source. CoreBluetooth scans actively and merges the scan response, so in practice this does not matter on macOS.
- Whether the Myo advertises any manufacturer-specific data is not stated in any source read. None of the libraries uses it.

## GATT layout and byte formats (myohw.h)

### Takeaway
All Myo-specific UUIDs are `d506XXXX-a904-deb9-4748-2c7f4a124842`. The Control service (0001) holds the info (0101), firmware (0201) and command (0401) characteristics. Each command is `[cmd_id, payload_len, payload…]` written to 0401. The battery level is the standard 0x180F/0x2A19 pair, and the device name is the standard 0x2A00. The header says "All values are big-endian", but every working implementation reads and writes **little-endian**, so use little-endian.

### Cited Findings
- Services and characteristics (short UUID → full d506XXXX-…): ControlService 0x0001; MyoInfoCharacteristic 0x0101 ("Serial number for this Myo and various parameters… Read-only"); FirmwareVersionCharacteristic 0x0201 (read-only); CommandCharacteristic 0x0401 ("Write-only"); ImuDataService 0x0002 with IMUDataCharacteristic 0x0402 (notify) and MotionEventCharacteristic 0x0502 (indicate); ClassifierService 0x0003 with ClassifierEventCharacteristic 0x0103 (indicate); EmgDataService 0x0005 with EmgData0–3 at 0x0105/0x0205/0x0305/0x0405 (notify); BatteryService 0x180F with BatteryLevelCharacteristic 0x2A19 ("Read/notify"); DeviceName 0x2A00 ("Read/write"). — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h)
- Undocumented characteristic 0x0104 in service 0x0004: "FV" or "filtered 50 Hz" EMG, 17 bytes `<8Hb`, enabled with EMG mode 1. It is used by pyomyo, pymyo (`EMG_SMOOTH`), FreeMyo and dl-myo. — [OSA protocol.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/protocol.md); [pymyo myo.py](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/blob/main/pymyo/myo.py); [FreeMyo myo_config.yaml](https://github.com/jshahbazi/FreeMyo.py/blob/main/myo_config.yaml)
- MyoInfo (`myohw_fw_info_t`, 20 bytes): serial_number[6], unlock_pose u16, active_classifier_type u8, active_classifier_index u8, has_custom_classifier u8, stream_indicating u8, sku u8 (0 unknown/old firmware, 1 black, 2 white), reserved[7]. pymyo parses it with `struct.unpack("<6sH5B7x", …)`. — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h); [pymyo myo.py](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/blob/main/pymyo/myo.py)
- FirmwareVersion (`myohw_fw_version_t`, 8 bytes): major, minor, patch, hardware_rev, each u16 (hardware_rev 1 = REV-C Alpha, 2 = REV-D retail). pymyo parses it as `<4H`. "Minor version is incremented for changes in this interface." The header documents interface `MYOHW_FIRMWARE_VERSION_MAJOR 1`, `MINOR 2`. — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h); [pymyo myo.py](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/blob/main/pymyo/myo.py)
- Command IDs: set_mode 0x01, vibrate 0x03, deep_sleep 0x04, vibrate2 0x07, set_sleep_mode 0x09, unlock 0x0A, user_action 0x0B. — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h). Undocumented set_led 0x06 (payload logo r,g,b + bar r,g,b) is used by dl-myo (`LED`, cmd 0x06) and pymyo (`struct.pack("<8B", 6, 6, *logo_rgb, *status_rgb)`). — [dl-myo myo/commands.py](https://github.com/iomz/dl-myo/blob/main/myo/commands.py); [pymyo myo.py](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/blob/main/pymyo/myo.py)
- Exact byte layouts (header = cmd byte + payload_size byte):
  - set_mode: `01 03 emg imu classifier` (5 bytes). EMG: 0x00 none, 0x02 filtered, 0x03 raw (0x01 = undocumented FV/50 Hz). IMU: 0x00 none, 0x01 data, 0x02 events, 0x03 all, 0x04 raw. Classifier: 0x00 disabled ("Disable and reset the internal state"), 0x01 enabled. — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h)
  - vibrate: `03 01 type` (type 0 none, 1 short, 2 medium, 3 long). — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h)
  - deep_sleep: `04 00` (2 bytes). — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h)
  - vibrate2: `07 12` followed by 6 steps × (duration u16 ms, strength u8 0–255), 20 bytes in total. The sources disagree on the length byte: pymyo sends 20, OSA 18 (the header says payload_size 18), and dl-myo packs the duration high byte first. — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h); [pymyo myo.py](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/blob/main/pymyo/myo.py); [dl-myo commands.py](https://github.com/iomz/dl-myo/blob/main/myo/commands.py); [OSA protocol.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/protocol.md)
  - set_sleep_mode: `09 01 mode` (0 = normal, "Myo will sleep after a period of inactivity"; 1 = "Never go to sleep"). pymyo: `struct.pack("<3B", 9, 1, sleep_mode)`. — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h); [pymyo myo.py](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/blob/main/pymyo/myo.py)
  - unlock: `0A 01 type` (0 lock, 1 timed, 2 hold). user_action: `0B 01 00` (single). — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h)
- Data payloads: IMU 20 bytes = quaternion w,x,y,z (int16, ÷16384), accelerometer[3] (÷2048, g), gyroscope[3] (÷16, deg/s), default rate 50 Hz. EMG 16 bytes = two 8-channel int8 samples, 200 Hz. Classifier event: type (1 arm_synced, 2 arm_unsynced, 3 pose, 4 unlocked, 5 locked, 6 sync_failed), then data. Poses: 0 rest, 1 fist, 2 wave_in, 3 wave_out, 4 fingers_spread, 5 double_tap, 0xFFFF unknown. — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h)
- Endianness conflict: the header says "All values are big-endian". OSA notes "header says big-endian; all implementations LE" and decides on LE, with "firmware reads 1.5.x.y, quaternion norm ≈ 1" as the hardware check. It also notes that the band sends 6-byte classifier events where the header defines 3, and lists an extra type 7 `warmup_completed` taken from libmyo. — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h); [OSA protocol.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/protocol.md)
- Write commands with response. OSA and pymyo use `write_gatt_char(…, response=True)`. OSA spaces command writes 20 ms apart (taken from MioConnect) and re-sends never-sleep every 10 s as a keep-alive (taken from myonnaise). — [OSA protocol.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/protocol.md); [pymyo myo.py](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/blob/main/pymyo/myo.py)
- The thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth repo's last code change was 2015-09-28 ("Fixing incorrect MotionEventCharacteristic UUID"); in 2020 it was relicensed (Apache-2.0, "Copyright 2015 Google LLC"). — [myo-bluetooth commits](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/commits/master)

### Inferences
- Minimum useful sequence after connecting: read 0x2A19 for battery and 0201 for firmware, write `09 01 01` (never sleep) so the band stays awake during the session, then `01 03 …` to start streams and subscribe to the characteristics. Write `09 01 00` on exit so the band can sleep again.
- Avoid `04 00` (deep sleep) in experiments. After it, only USB power brings the band back.

### Gaps
- No Thalmic-published spec for the undocumented FV characteristic (0x0104) or for set_led (0x06). Both are known only from the libraries.

## Minimum firmware for direct BLE use without the dongle

### Takeaway
The published protocol documents interface version 1.2, and every library is tested against the last firmware, 1.5.1970. Bands on older firmware may fail on some reads. Firmware can be updated with no Myo Connect: plug the band in, and it mounts as a USB drive named "Myo"; copy the HEX file onto it.

### Cited Findings
- The header defines `MYOHW_FIRMWARE_VERSION_MAJOR 1` and `MYOHW_FIRMWARE_VERSION_MINOR 2`; "Minor version is incremented for changes in this interface." — [myohw.h](https://github.com/thalmiclabs/myo-bluetooth/blob/master/myohw.h)
- pymyo's maintainer: "The only Myo firmware version I have access to and tested with different Myos is `1.5.1970` which is, to my knowledge, the last firmware version released… only the version 1.2 and above is public." A user with older firmware could not read the info characteristic until they upgraded; reading the name still failed for them. — [pymyo issue #5](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/issues/5)
- OSA's hardware checklist expects `fw=1.5.xxxx (rev_d)` on a retail unit and says that "Any other number means byte order or struct layout is wrong". — [OSA hardware-tests.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/hardware-tests.md)
- pyomyo has comments about different command sequences for "v1.0 firmware" (raw data vs. poses). — [pyomyo source](https://github.com/PerlinWarp/pyomyo/blob/main/src/pyomyo/pyomyo.py)
- Manual firmware update: connect by USB → a drive called "Myo" mounts → copy the firmware HEX file → the drive remounts with SUCCESS.txt → disconnect; the LED pulses pale purple while the update finishes. "Updating the firmware is the same process for Windows and Mac OS X". — [Thalmic support: manual firmware update (Wayback 2018)](http://web.archive.org/web/20190717164610/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/201763319-How-do-I-update-the-firmware-on-my-Myo-armband-)
- The 1.5.1970 HEX file that pymyo's maintainer points to is now hosted on a third-party mirror (Softpedia). — [pymyo issue #5](https://github.com/Crimson-Crow/pymyo/issues/5)

### Inferences
- Plugging a band into the Mac and checking whether a "Myo" volume mounts is a cheap health check, independent of Bluetooth. If it mounts, the band's MCU is alive, and the firmware can be reloaded if needed.
- A HEX file from a third-party mirror is an untrusted download. Check its provenance (for example against a hash from an archived Thalmic downloads page) before flashing.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives an official "minimum firmware for direct BLE" figure. Thalmic's protocol release post was not retrievable.
- The official Thalmic firmware download page was not located in the Wayback index during this pass.

## Open-source libraries that connect without the dongle

### Takeaway
On macOS today the two strongest options are **OSA MYO Controller** (macOS-first, Apple Silicon native, used with real Myos over the Mac's built-in Bluetooth, commits up to 2026-10-22) and **dl-myo** (bleak, claims macOS support, commits up to 2026-10-23). **pymyo** is a clean, small bleak API, last released mid-2024. Open-Myo is Linux-only, and pyomyo and myo-raw need the dongle.

### Cited Findings
- **OSA MYO Controller** (dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller, MIT; last commit 2026-10-22). "A driver, monitor and OSC bridge for the Myo… on macOS", "Native on Apple Silicon. Built-in Bluetooth (CoreBluetooth) or the BLED112 USB dongle", needs macOS 12+ and Python 3.11+. Commands: `osa-myo scan`, `dump`, `run`. Status: "Used with physical Myos over the Mac's built-in Bluetooth. The BLED112 dongle backend and the web version are tested against simulated hardware". Discovery: `BleakScanner.discover(timeout, return_adv=True)`, matched on the control UUID in `adv.service_uuids` or "myo" in the name. It selects a device by CoreBluetooth UUID, name, or serial. ### Inferences
- For the first smoke test on the Mac Mini, `osa-myo scan` (run from Terminal.app or iTerm) is the most direct check, because it matches on UUID or name and was built and used on Apple Silicon. A three-line bleak script using pymyo's filter (`BleakScanner.discover(service_uuids=[...])`) is the minimal alternative.
- dl-myo's discovery relies on bleak's undocumented `retrieveAddressForPeripheral_` path on macOS. That is a plausible breakage point on macOS 26, but not verified. Prefer a filter without `use_bdaddr` when debugging.

### Gaps
- No library documents a test on macOS 26 specifically.
- I did not read pymyo, dl-myo or OSA end to end. The discovery and command paths were read; the streaming code was only skimmed.

## macOS specifics (addresses, permission, known problems)

### Takeaway
CoreBluetooth gives each peripheral a per-Mac UUID, not its MAC address, so libraries select by that UUID, by name, or by the serial number read after connecting. Any process that uses CoreBluetooth must be started from an app that declares Bluetooth usage, or TCC kills it. The user's scan saw other devices, so permission is not the problem here. The known Myo-on-Mac failure is a "stale pairing": forget the Myo in System Settings → Bluetooth and toggle Bluetooth off and on. No source reports a pairing prompt or a need for encryption.

### Cited Findings
- OSA: "Any process using CoreBluetooth must be launched by an app that declares `NSBluetoothAlwaysUsageDescription`; otherwise TCC kills it with SIGABRT. Terminal.app and iTerm2 do". Its checklist says to run "not from an IDE or the Claude desktop app's shell", and that the symptom is "a silent crash on `scan`". — [OSA protocol.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/protocol.md); [OSA hardware-tests.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/hardware-tests.md); [OSA guide.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/guide.md)
- OSA: "CoreBluetooth caches GATT databases; after a firmware change forget the device in System Settings → Bluetooth." On repeated connect timeouts: "macOS holds a stale pairing: System Settings → Bluetooth, forget the Myo if listed; toggle Bluetooth off/on. Old trick from FreeMyo: let the band's battery run flat to clear its own bond table." — [OSA protocol.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/protocol.md); [OSA guide.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/guide.md)
- OSA's reason for supporting the BLED112 at all: "CoreBluetooth hides MAC addresses and sometimes keeps a stale pairing to a Myo; the dongle gives you the real MAC, a second independent radio". — [OSA bled112_transport.py](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/src/myo_bridge/transport/bled112_transport.py)
- The MAC address is printed on the bottom of the Myo's box (diagnostics.myo.com, the other source, is gone). The 6-byte `serial_number` in MyoInfo is compared with that MAC in OSA's checklist; OSA calls its display byte order "a guess". — [Thalmic support: Finding the MAC address (Wayback 2019)](http://web.archive.org/web/20190719061207/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/207523646-Finding-the-MAC-address-of-a-Myo-armband); [OSA hardware-tests.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/hardware-tests.md)
- Thalmic officially required its dongle ("to ensure compatibility across multiple PC manufacturers") but stated: "Developers can create their own Bluetooth connection implementation to connect to built-in Bluetooth adapters using the Bluetooth Protocol." — [Thalmic support: Can I use my computer's built-in Bluetooth? (Wayback 2019)](http://web.archive.org/web/20190717164800/https://support.getmyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/203996655-Can-I-use-my-computers-built-in-Bluetooth-)
- bleak exposes indicate-type characteristics (classifier 0103, motion 0502) through `start_notify`. — [OSA protocol.md](https://github.com/dnlfbr/OSA-MYO-Controller/blob/main/docs/protocol.md)
- dl-myo issue #22 is a CoreBluetooth-backend error (`services` is None) when a command is written after the link has dropped. — [dl-myo issue #22](https://github.com/iomz/dl-myo/issues/22)

### Inferences
- Since bleak saw 5–9 devices, the TCC permission and the radio both work. The remaining explanations, in order of likelihood: (1) the bands were plugged in or charging, which is the designed non-operational state; (2) the bands were asleep or in deep sleep and were not moved after unplugging; (3) the advertising window passed before the scan; (4) the bands are flat or have faulted firmware (check the "Myo" USB drive and the LED colours).
- A stale CoreBluetooth pairing shows up as *connect* timeouts, not as an empty *scan*. It is unlikely to explain the empty scan, and is worth checking only once a band shows up in a scan.

### Gaps
- No source reports a Myo-specific CoreBluetooth regression on macOS 15 or 26.
- Whether any old Myo Connect for macOS left a system-level pairing record is unknown; it used the dongle, not the built-in radio, so probably not.
